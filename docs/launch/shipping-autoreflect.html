READ THIS ONE FIRST

Your first tenet stops at the edge of the device

Never lose what the user said or wrote. The code honours that: explicit flushes, audio durable before transcription, resumable sync, a migration suite that opens real stores. All of it survives process death, interrupted syncs and restore-from-backup.

None of it survives a lost phone. There is no account, no cloud copy, no export. And the person who loses everything will have chosen you because you promised they wouldn't.

It is invisible from inside the repo, because the code is correct. It is glaring from outside it.

IDENTITY

What this actually is

Nothing below is a task. It is the thing you have to be able to say before the listing, the screenshots and half the roadmap can be written.

Why it is hard to name

The journal does not live in this app. It lives in Notion. That makes AutoReflect a thing that sits in front of another product, and those are always harder to name than products that simply are a thing. "A notes app" needs no explanation; "the place your notes start before they reach your notes app" does.

Solved problem, good precedent. Drafts holds exactly this position for text and named itself out of the corner in four words — Where Text Starts. It does not claim to be your writing app. It claims to be the doorway. That framing built a real business in a category everyone called full.

So the category is a capture app: fast, forgiving, and pointed somewhere else. Say the destination out loud — naming Notion is what makes a stranger understand you in one sentence instead of three.

And the paste-able one, when a model or a spec needs the mechanics:

AutoReflect is an iOS journaling app that acts as a capture layer in front of Notion. You speak or type an entry; the audio is saved to disk before transcription is even attempted, so nothing is ever lost. It structures your text into a bullet hierarchy without changing your words — enforced by a word-level diff, not just a prompt. Photos and videos are routed to the day they were taken. It shows which days still have no entry, checked against both the phone and the Notion database. Finished entries sync to Notion as their own page, resumably and idempotently. The journal lives in Notion; the app is the fast, reliable front door to it.

The trap to avoid

"Never lose your work" is the truest thing about this app and the weakest thing to lead with. Every competitor implicitly claims it, a stranger cannot verify it from a listing, and it is only felt on the day it saves them. It is a reason to stay, not a reason to try.

Lead with something visible: assembly, not storage. The fastest path from "something happened" to "it is written down, on the right day, with the photos". Photo-import-by-date and the open-days check make that literal, and together they are a fifteen-second demo.

Knowing whether it landed

"How does it know which day the photos belong to?" — you have them. The demo follows naturally.

"Oh, like Day One?" — you have lost them. Lead harder on Notion. Day One is a destination; you are a doorway.

DECIDE

Open questions

Choices, not chores — which is why none of these have a checkbox. Each one changes what the tasks below even are, so they come first.

What shape does 1.0 ship in?

Blocks everything

The backend today is honestly single-tenant: one Notion token in one JSON file, one shared Groq key, an in-memory idempotency store. Fine for one person, cannot serve strangers.

A / No backend On-device transcription and formatting; Notion OAuth via a thin stateless proxy. Zero per-user cost, no servers to run or breach. AI quality drops; real work to swap providers.
B / Bring your own key Users paste their own Groq key. Near-zero cost, brutal onboarding, caps you at a few hundred technical users.
C / Full SaaS Multi-tenant, accounts, subscriptions, you pay for inference. Months of work and real liability — and the only shape that becomes a business.

Leaning A — your thesis is local-first, and the most aligned version has no server at all

Do you add analytics?

Trade-off

You need to know where people fall out of onboarding. But the moment you add analytics, "data not collected" stops being a truthful answer on Apple's privacy questionnaire — and for a journaling app that answer is one of the strongest things you can say.

If yes: events only. Never entry text, titles, transcripts, or counts specific enough to fingerprint a person.

Company, or your own name?

Before you charge

Shipping as an individual puts your legal name on the listing, publicly. An LLC costs a few hundred dollars and separates personal assets from a product holding other people's private journals — worth more here than usual.

What does it cost, and what does that cost you?

After shape

On path C every dictated minute costs you money. Measure your own usage for a month and price against a heavy user, not an average one — journalers are not average, and the people who love this most will use it daily.

A flat subscription against a variable cost is how indie apps quietly lose money on their best customers. On path A the problem does not exist.

AVOID

Deliberately not doing

Not tasks. Things that feel like progress and are not — listed so that skipping them is a decision rather than an oversight.

  • Paid ads before you know who converts.
  • Press releases. No journalist reads them.
  • Influencer outreach for an app at this price.
  • A Discord server with no members in it.
  • Product Hunt as an opening move. It rewards an audience you already have — it is a second launch, not a first.
  • A second deep integration. Export answers "I don't use Notion" for most people; being an integration layer is a commodity position, one roadmap item from being absorbed.
  • Rebuilding the landing page a fourth time because it is more comfortable than talking to people. This is the one that will actually get you — polishing is work that feels productive and reaches nobody.
