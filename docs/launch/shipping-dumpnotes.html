What DumpNotes is, and how to say it

The sentence:

DumpNotes is an app that lets you quickly capture thoughts, moments, and experiences through text, voice, images, or video, and automatically adds them to your Notion journals.

Still needed: a subtitle. The App Store caps it at 30 characters, so the sentence above is the description's opening line, not the subtitle. "Dump anything. Save everything." is 31 — one over.

Tagline: Dump anything. Save everything.

One practical note: the App Store subtitle is capped at 30 characters, so that sentence is the opening line of the description, not the subtitle. The subtitle needs its own short phrase.

Why the category was hard to name

The journal does not live in this app. It lives in Notion. Products that sit in front of another product are always harder to name than products that are a thing.

Drafts solved the same problem in four words — Where Text Starts. It does not claim to be your writing app, it claims to be the doorway. DumpNotes is the tray.

Knowing whether it landed

"So it just goes straight into Notion?" — you have them.

"Oh, like Day One?" — you have lost them. Lead harder on Notion.

Decisions already made

Shape: no backend. On-device transcription and formatting; Notion OAuth through a thin stateless proxy. Zero per-user cost, no server to run or breach. The open question is only whether Apple's on-device speech is good enough to replace Groq — testable in an afternoon.

Analytics: no. "Data not collected" stays truthful on the privacy questionnaire, which is one of the strongest things a journaling app can say. Feedback route shipped in its place.

Company: later. One LLC can publish any number of apps, so a studio entity holding several is the normal shape and waiting for revenue is reasonable. Verify before shipping under your own name: converting an Apple individual account to an organization later involves Apple, a D-U-N-S number, and re-signing agreements.

Data model: unchanged. A dump is an EntryDraft, and several per day with their own Notion page has been supported since ADR-006 was revised. The capture-first rebuild needed no migration.

Deliberately not doing
  • Paid ads before you know who converts.
  • Press releases. No journalist reads them.
  • Product Hunt as an opening move. It rewards an audience you already have.
  • A second deep integration. Export answers "I don't use Notion" for most people, and being an integration layer is a commodity position.
  • Rebuilding the landing page a fourth time because it is more comfortable than talking to people. This is the one that will actually get you.
The one thing that could still bite

Everything lives on one device. Export shipped, so the journal can now leave the phone — but it is a manual action. There is still no account and no cloud copy, so a lost phone with no recent export loses everything.

The schema was deliberately kept CloudKit-compatible (ADR-008b), so that door is open whenever you want it.

Most people ship the features and find the foundations later.
