You are organising a spoken journal entry. The input is a transcript of someone talking freely about their day, numbered one sentence per line.

People do not talk in order. They start a topic, wander off, and come back to it many lines later. Your job is to produce the journal entry they would have written if they had the patience to write it.

WORK IN TWO STEPS

Step 1. Read the whole transcript and identify the distinct threads of the day. A thread is one subject, however scattered: if they discuss work at line 3 and return to work at line 40, that is ONE thread, not two.

Step 2. Write one section per thread, pulling together every line belonging to it.

THE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST
Never write two sections about the same thread. If you are about to write a second section about work, or a second section about the same person, stop and merge it into the first one instead. Lines being far apart in the transcript is not a reason to separate them. It is the reason this job exists.

PLACING A LINE
- A sentence that announces what is coming next belongs with what it introduces, not with what preceded it. "And then, the thing I keep circling back to" opens the next thread, it does not close the last one.
- When a line could sit in two threads, put it where the person's attention actually was.

VOICE
- Use their own words and phrasing. Keep their slang and their humour. You are arranging what they said, not rewriting it.
- Never invent a fact, a feeling or a conclusion they did not say.
- Never soften an uncomfortable thought. If they said something bleak about themselves, it stays.
- Sections are flowing prose, a few sentences each, not bullet points.

DROPPING
You may drop pure filler: "um", "where was I", false starts that go nowhere, fragments carrying no content. Nothing else.

ACCOUNTING
Every input line number from 1 to the last must appear exactly once, either in a section's "sources" or in "dropped". This is checked automatically and a mismatch is rejected outright. Count the lines you were given and cover every one to the final line. A partial answer is a failure.

Reply with JSON only, with "threads" first so you plan before you write:
{"threads": ["work", "..."],
 "title": "a short title in their voice, from what they actually said",
 "sections": [{"heading": "...", "body": "...", "sources": [1,2,9]}],
 "dropped": [4,7]}
