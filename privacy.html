DumpNotes

Privacy Policy

Last updated 3 September 2026

DumpNotes is a journal that lives on your phone. This policy explains what it does with what you write, and it is short because the app does very little.

The short version. There is no account. There is no analytics and no tracking of any kind. Everything you capture is stored on your device, and none of it is sent anywhere unless you connect the app to a Notion database yourself.

Who is responsible

DumpNotes is made and published by Samuel Osa-Agbontaen, an individual developer. There is no company, no team, and nobody else with access to anything described below.

What is stored on your device

Everything the app is for:

  • The text you type and any titles you give an entry.
  • Photos and videos you attach.
  • Voice recordings, and any transcript produced from them.
  • The date each entry is filed under, and when it was created and last edited.
  • Your settings, such as appearance and which font your entries are shown in.

This is held in the app's own storage on the device. It is included in your normal encrypted iPhone backup, which is how a journal survives onto a new phone. It is not uploaded to us, because there is no us to upload it to.

What is not collected

  • No account. There is nothing to sign up for and no profile.
  • No analytics. No usage statistics, no crash reporting, no session recording, no advertising identifiers, no third-party SDKs of any kind.
  • No location. The app never asks for it and never reads it.
  • No contacts, no calendar, no health data.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and nothing is sold or shared with data brokers.

The app contains exactly one piece of code capable of making a network request, and it is inactive unless you configure a server address yourself. As distributed, no server address is set.

If you connect Notion

Sending entries to Notion is optional and off by default. It requires you to run or connect a server and sign in to Notion. If you choose to do that, then and only then:

  • The content of an entry you send, including its text and any attached photos or videos, is transmitted to that server and on to Notion so that it can be written into the database you picked.
  • If you use dictation, the audio is transmitted to that server to be turned into text.
  • If you use the formatting feature, the entry's text is transmitted to that server to be restructured. The wording is never changed, only its arrangement.

Where that server is configured to use them, transcription and formatting are performed by Groq, and storage is performed by Notion. Their handling of that content is governed by their own policies. Nothing is sent to either of them unless you have connected them.

Your Notion authorisation is held by the server you connected, in a file readable only by that server's own account, and it is never sent back to the app. The app only ever learns whether a connection exists, the workspace name, and which database you chose.

Feedback

If you use the feedback option in Settings, the app opens your own mail app with a message you can read and edit before sending. It attaches only the app version, your iOS version, and your device model, so that a bug report is actionable. It never attaches any part of an entry: not text, not titles, not photos, not recordings.

Keeping and deleting your journal

  • Entries stay on your device until you delete them.
  • Deleting an entry removes its text and its attached files from the device.
  • Deleting the app removes all of it. There is no copy held anywhere else.
  • You can export the whole journal, or any entries you select, as plain markdown files with the media beside them. Nothing in an export needs this app to read it.
  • Anything already written into Notion stays in Notion. Deleting it there is done in Notion, and deleting an entry in DumpNotes does not remove the Notion page.

Children

DumpNotes is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from them. Since the app collects nothing at all, there is nothing held about any user of any age.

Your rights

Laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export and delete personal data held about you. In this case those rights are satisfied by the app itself: the data is on your device, you can read and edit all of it, the export feature gives you a complete portable copy, and deleting the app destroys it. There is no server-side record to request, because none is kept.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the previous versions remain in the app's public source history. If a change ever means the app collects something it did not collect before, that will be said plainly here before it ships.
